# Grafting means foreign genetics, not brownfield

We had been using *grafting* as the name for all work on an existing repository, with *sowing* for greenfield. That is wrong in both directions. Botanically, a graft joins tissue from a **different plant** onto a rootstock; a plant extending itself is simply growing, and inherits its own genetics by definition. Using *grafting* for brownfield makes the most common act in software engineering — adding a feature to an existing codebase in that codebase's own idiom — sound exotic and risky, while hiding what is actually risky: tissue whose genetics are not the plant's own. We therefore split the two: **growing** is the plant extending itself and inheriting; **grafting** is joining foreign tissue and requiring a union, because foreign genetics cannot be inherited, only translated.

**Consequences:** *brownfield* is not a synonym for grafting and should not appear in the vocabulary. Intake asks "whose genetics?" rather than "new repo or existing repo?". A growing leaf is required to read the plant before extending it; a grafting leaf is required to build the union explicitly. The rootstock / scion / union / sucker family stays parked until integration work is actually built, because those are the parts of a graft, not the act itself.

**Considered options:** grafting as brownfield (rejected: mislabels the default case, and hides the real risk); growth as the only mode with no graft term (rejected: integrations are genuinely different in kind, and without a name their union points are left implicit and leak).
