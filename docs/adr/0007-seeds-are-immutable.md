# Seeds are immutable; genetics change by generation, not by patch

A seed that can be edited in place is not a seed but a moving target. Every plant grown from it becomes undocumented — you can no longer say which instructions produced which plant — and a silent edit retroactively changes the meaning of every past sowing. We therefore treat seeds as immutable artifacts in a seed bank. When the genetics genuinely change, a **new generation** is released and the old one stays. Patches do not exist: a diff between two generations is the patch.

Grafting does not produce a generation. A graft joins tissue from a different plant, so the plant becomes a **chimera** — two genomes in one organism, joined at a union — while its own genetics are untouched. Recording a graft therefore writes to the plant's **journal**, not to its seed. If a graft becomes part of how the variety is always grown, that is a new generation of the seed, not an edit to the old one.

**Consequences:** every plant carries two provenance artifacts — its seed generation and its journal. The seed bank is a lineage, not a folder. Anything that scaffolds from a seed must pin a generation, or the plant it produces is unreproducible. Only two kinds of act create a generation: a mutation, and seed saving. Seed saving has two forms — harvesting the genetics of a plant that survived a full cycle, and folding a proven graft into the variety.

**Considered options:** mutable seeds (rejected: silent genetic drift, undocumented plants, no reproducibility); grafts as seed patches (rejected: grafting does not change the plant's genetics, so a patch would record a change that did not happen — and would make the graft look inheritable when it is not).
